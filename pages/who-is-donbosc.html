The Biography of saint Don Bosco

John Bosco was born on August 16, 1815, in a small village called Becchi, near Turin, Italy. He was the youngest child of a poor farmer named Francesco Bosco and his wife, Margherita. John's father died when he was just two years old, leaving his mother to raise him and his two older brothers on her own.

Despite the family's poverty, John's mother instilled in him a deep faith and a love for learning. John was a bright and curious child who loved to explore the world around him. He was particularly fascinated by the work of traveling performers and acrobats who would pass through his village.

When John was nine years old, he had a dream that would change the course of his life. In the dream, he saw a group of boys who were fighting and cursing. He tried to intervene and calm them down, but they only became more violent. Suddenly, a person appeared and told John to win the boys over with kindness and love. This dream stayed with John throughout his life and inspired him to dedicate his life to helping troubled youth.

John's mother encouraged him to pursue his education, but the family's poverty made it difficult for him to attend school regularly. Despite this, John was determined to learn and would often walk long distances to attend classes. He also worked odd jobs to pay for his tuition.

When John was 12 years old, he had an encounter with a priest named Joseph Cafasso that would change his life forever. Father Cafasso took an interest in John and became his mentor. He helped John to continue his education and encouraged him to pursue a vocation in the priesthood.

In 1835, John entered the seminary in Chieri, Italy, where he continued his studies. He was ordained a priest in 1841 and began working with young people in Turin. He quickly gained a reputation for his kindness and compassion towards troubled youth.

In 1859, John founded the Salesian Society, an organization dedicated to helping disadvantaged youth through education and vocational training. The Salesians grew rapidly and spread throughout Europe and eventually to other parts of the world.

John Bosco died on January 31, 1888, at the age of 72. He was canonized as a saint by Pope Pius XI in 1934. Today, Saint John Bosco is remembered as a champion of youth and a tireless advocate for education and social justice.
